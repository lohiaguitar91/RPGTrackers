The shape of it

Three campaigns, one hub

A new game runs a single Oak intro (gender, name, outfit, and a one-time difficulty choice) and drops you in the World Transit hub, an Indigo-Plateau-styled terminal. Four staffed gates lead to Kanto, Johto, Hoenn and the Battle Frontier. Each region then plays its own canonical opening, including a choice of its three starters.

The hub also holds a nurse, a storage PC, a mart carrying twelve ball types, and a World Tour board reading your running badge total out of 24.

Getting around

  • Hub gates box your current party to the shared PC so each campaign starts fresh. Nothing is lost, and the crossing is refused up front if the boxes can't take the whole team rather than half-boxing you.
  • The HUB PASS key item warps you back to the hub from the bag. One-way, so you re-enter through the gates. Every gate attendant force-gives it before letting you cross, so you can't strand yourself.
  • Each region has an in-world access point the hub returns you to on later visits: the Goldenrod Magnet Train, Vermilion City, and the Slateport harbor.

Johto → Kanto is the exception changed here

The sea crossing is the one route that isn't a hub gate. Elm's S.S. Ticket sends the new Johto Champion out of Olivine harbor; you disembark at Vermilion City port with your party still with you. HG/SS treats the two regions as one journey.

Upstream, badges still reset at the shore, so a level-45 team arrived in Kanto counting as zero badges: disobedient, and unable to use Cut. In this build badges count across all regions, so obedience, level caps, catch rates and HM access carry over. Kanto's gyms sit at Lv 58–65 against Clair's 42–45, so the content always assumed continuity even when the plumbing didn't.

Practical effect: play Johto first, then sail. The ticket gating enforces this anyway.

Difficulty

Normal and Hard

Hard Mode means Set-style battles, no bag items in trainer battles, and badge-based level caps. Normal removes all three, including the level caps entirely.

Upstream, this was asked once during Oak's speech and then locked to that save forever. This build moves it into the Options menu as DIFFICULTY, so you can switch whenever you like. changed here

Difficulty is otherwise contained per region: being Hoenn Champion doesn't harden Kanto's first-run gyms.

The roster

Gen 1–3, families intact

Every wild, gift and trainer Pokémon comes from the first three generations. Those lines keep their later-gen evolutions — Togekiss, Electivire, Weavile, Sylveon and the rest — with the cross-gen evolution items sold in-world, and they count toward the National Dex.

This is enforced rather than a style guide: all 339 Gen 4–9 families are compiled out, and a validator fails the build if any obtainable content references one.

Everything is catchable solo verified here

No trading, no distribution events, one save file. All 417 of 417 dex species have a reachable acquisition path, checked on every build.

  • Trade evolutions work offline via a Linking Cord.
  • Event mythicals are handed out in-world — the Old Sea Map, Aurora Ticket, Mystic Ticket, Eon Ticket and GS Ball all come from scripts, unlocking Mew, Deoxys, Lugia, Ho-Oh, Latias, Latios and Celebi.
  • Jirachi was the one species with no home anywhere. It has one now.

This build

Changes on top of upstream

Catching is easier

CATCH RATE in Options now reads 1x / 1.5x / 2x / 6x. The unused 0.5x slot was repurposed rather than widening the save format, so the 6x tier costs nothing.

At 6x, a Lv 70 legendary at 1 HP and asleep in an Ultra Ball goes from roughly 4.4% to 28% per throw — about 4 balls instead of 23. Mid-tier catching stops being a mechanic. EXP RATE keeps its own separate 0.5x.

Jirachi has an encounter

A bonus-disc exclusive in retail, and absent from upstream entirely — it appeared only in flavour text. It now stands in Meteor Falls B1F 2R at Lv 40, on the far ledge across the chamber's water, and appears once you're Hoenn Champion. Bring Surf. One-shot: it's gone once caught.

Two Pokémon that were uncatchable

Roselia and Surskit had zero wild encounter entries anywhere, which silently took Roserade, Budew and Masquerain with them — five species you could never have obtained in a build whose whole premise is completeness.

Both are back on their RSE-native routes: Roselia on Routes 117, 119 and 120; Surskit on 102 and 117. Each took a slot held by a species that appears on many other tables, so nothing else lost its only home.

The Kanto handoff, and the difficulty toggle

Covered above — badges now count across regions, and Hard Mode moved into Options.

Tin Tower has a roof again

Upstream, Tin Tower cannot be climbed. Walking from the front door reaches 1F, 2F and eighteen tiles of 3F, then stops — Ho-Oh, the five Kimono Girls and every item above were unreachable.

The cause is structural: floors 3F through 6F each have every walkable region holding exactly one staircase, so you arrive and the only warp in your region is the one under your feet, which sends you back. Bad collision, missing stairs and wrong warp indices were each ruled out.

Since 7F, 8F and 9F are sound, 2F's up-staircase now leads to 7F. Floors 3F–6F stay unreachable exactly as they already were, so nothing is lost, and the roof, Ho-Oh, the Kimono Girls, Max Revive, Full Restore, Max Elixir and the Big Nugget are all reachable again.

Caveats

Known issues

Tin Tower 3F–6F are still dead

The staircase reroute is a stopgap, not a repair. Those four floors remain unreachable, and with them a Full Heal, two Max Potions, an Escape Rope, an Ultra Ball, a Rare Candy and a Carbos. Properly fixing it means redrawing four floors. Nothing unique is behind them — the wild tables there are Gastly and Rattata.

Ho-Oh has a second source anyway

Independent of Tin Tower, Ho-Oh and Lugia are both reachable at Navel Rock via the Mystic Ticket, so the dex was never at risk.
