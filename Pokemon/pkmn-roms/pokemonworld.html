The shape of it

Three campaigns, one hub

A new game runs a single Oak intro (gender, name, outfit, and a one-time difficulty choice) and drops you in the World Transit hub, an Indigo-Plateau-styled terminal. Four staffed gates lead to Kanto, Johto, Hoenn and the Battle Frontier. Each region then plays its own canonical opening, including a choice of its three starters.

The hub also holds a nurse, a storage PC, a mart carrying twelve ball types, and a World Tour board reading your running badge total out of 24.

Getting around

  • Hub gates box your current party to the shared PC so each campaign starts fresh. Nothing is lost, and the crossing is refused up front if the boxes can't take the whole team rather than half-boxing you.
  • The HUB PASS key item warps you back to the hub from the bag. One-way, so you re-enter through the gates. Every gate attendant force-gives it before letting you cross, so you can't strand yourself.
  • Each region has an in-world access point the hub returns you to on later visits: the Goldenrod Magnet Train, Vermilion City, and the Slateport harbor.

Johto → Kanto is the exception changed here

The sea crossing is the one route that isn't a hub gate. Elm's S.S. Ticket sends the new Johto Champion out of Olivine harbor; you disembark at Vermilion City port with your party still with you. HG/SS treats the two regions as one journey.

Upstream, badges still reset at the shore, so a level-45 team arrived in Kanto counting as zero badges: disobedient, and unable to use Cut. In this build badges count across all regions, so obedience, level caps, catch rates and HM access carry over. Kanto's gyms sit at Lv 58–65 against Clair's 42–45, so the content always assumed continuity even when the plumbing didn't.

Practical effect: play Johto first, then sail. The ticket gating enforces this anyway.

Difficulty

Normal and Hard

Hard Mode means Set-style battles, no bag items in trainer battles, and badge-based level caps. Normal removes all three, including the level caps entirely.

Difficulty is otherwise contained per region: being Hoenn Champion doesn't harden Kanto's first-run gyms.

The roster

Gen 1–3, families intact

Every wild, gift and trainer Pokémon comes from the first three generations. Those lines keep their later-gen evolutions — Togekiss, Electivire, Weavile, Sylveon and the rest — with the cross-gen evolution items sold in-world, and they count toward the National Dex.

This is enforced rather than a style guide: all 339 Gen 4–9 families are compiled out, and a validator fails the build if any obtainable content references one.

Everything is catchable solo verified here

No trading, no distribution events, one save file. All 417 of 417 dex species have a reachable acquisition path, checked on every build.

  • Trade evolutions work offline via a Linking Cord.
  • Event mythicals are handed out in-world — the Old Sea Map, Aurora Ticket, Mystic Ticket, Eon Ticket and GS Ball all come from scripts, unlocking Mew, Deoxys, Lugia, Ho-Oh, Latias, Latios and Celebi.
  • Jirachi was the one species with no home anywhere. It has one now.

Quality of life

Shiny hunting, DexNav, and skipping HM chores

HMs without teaching them

A party Pokémon that could learn an HM performs its field move — Cut, Flash, whatever — without ever having it taught into a move slot, as long as you have the region's badge for it. Owning the matching tool item unlocks the field move outright even without a compatible Pokémon. Either way it's a menu convenience, not a real learned move — if a compatible mon looks like it "already knows" an HM you never taught, this is why. HMs are also forgettable like any other move once actually learned for real.

Shiny odds

Base rate is 1 in 128 per encounter — already 64× the vanilla 1/8192, with nothing active. On a miss, a reroll draws a fresh independent attempt at the same odds, so odds compound as 1 − (127/128)^(1 + rerolls) rather than adding flat percentage points.

SourceRerolls
Shiny Charm+2, flat
Lure active (not Repel)+1, flat
Chain fishing streak+2 per streak point, caps at 20 (so +40 max)
DexNav chain+5 at chain ≥50, +10 at ≥100, plus a flat 4% shot at +4 regardless of chain

Purely cosmetic — no stat, IV or catch-rate difference from a normal encounter.

Shiny Charm source

The hub's Charm Curator hands out a badge-count ladder (your running total across all three regions, out of 24): 4 badges → Catching Charm, 8 → Exp. Charm, 12 → Oval Charm, 16 → Shiny Charm, 24 → an EV/IV Changer key item. It's a guaranteed pickup at that badge count, not a drop.

DexNav

Granted with each region's Pokédex; the hidden-Pokémon detector itself unlocks with your first regional championship. Two separate trackers: a per-species search level that raises encounter quality/rarity the more you DexNav that species, and a chain (caps at 100) built by chaining hidden encounters back to back, which is what feeds the shiny-reroll bonuses above. Hidden encounters are authored for nearly every land route in all three regions.

Chain fishing

Reeling in the same species back-to-back on a rod builds a streak, capped at 20, each point worth +2 shiny rerolls. The streak resets to zero the moment you get "not even a nibble," a fish "gets away," or you leave the fishing spot — so it has to be an unbroken run of catches in place.

This build

Changes on top of upstream

Catching is easier

CATCH RATE in Options now reads 1x / 1.5x / 2x / 6x. The unused 0.5x slot was repurposed rather than widening the save format, so the 6x tier costs nothing.

At 6x, a Lv 70 legendary at 1 HP and asleep in an Ultra Ball goes from roughly 4.4% to 28% per throw — about 4 balls instead of 23. Mid-tier catching stops being a mechanic. EXP RATE keeps its own separate 0.5x.

Seafoam Islands boulders actually fall through now

Pushing a Strength boulder into a hole on 1F/B1F/B2F/B3F played the fall sound and removed the boulder, but its counterpart never appeared on the floor below — the landing spot was just empty. The engine reads which flag to clear from the boulder's own data field, and all 8 fall-through boulders had that field pointing at nothing. Fixed at the data level, so the 1F→B1F→B2F→B3F→B4F chain works as intended.

Checked every other pushable boulder in the game (31 maps, all three regions) — Seafoam is the only place using this mechanic, so nothing else was affected. Save-compatible: no flag indices changed. Reported upstream as issue #2.

Both Dojo Hitmons are claimable

The Saffron Dojo's two prize balls shared one flag — taking Hitmonlee or Hitmonchan permanently locked the other. Each ball now guards on its own flag, so you take both, one each. Same fix for a narrow Eevee edge case: Bill's Goldenrod gift no longer shares a flag with the Celadon Condominiums ball.

Save-compatible: no flag indices moved. A save that already picked one Hitmon just walks back in and claims the other. The rest of the audit came back clean — fossils are backstopped in the Ruins of Alph and Desert Underpass, both Latis are obtainable, and the second Hitmon was always reachable the long way via Johto's Tyrogue. Reported upstream as issue #1.

Game Corner Pokémon now cost 1 coin

Both prize rooms — Celadon (Kanto) and Goldenrod (Johto) — sold Abra through Porygon for up to 9,999 coins. Every Pokémon in both rooms is now 1 coin: Abra, Clefairy, Dratini/Wobbuffet, Scyther, Porygon.

Pure script-data edit — a coin-price constant in a shop script, no flag, var, species or item renumbering. Existing saves load unaffected; the new price just applies next visit.

Jirachi has an encounter

A bonus-disc exclusive in retail, and absent from upstream entirely — it appeared only in flavour text. It now stands in Meteor Falls B1F 2R at Lv 40, on the far ledge across the chamber's water, and appears once you're Hoenn Champion. Bring Surf. One-shot: it's gone once caught.

Two Pokémon that were uncatchable

Roselia and Surskit had zero wild encounter entries anywhere, which silently took Roserade, Budew and Masquerain with them — five species you could never have obtained in a build whose whole premise is completeness.

Both are back on their RSE-native routes: Roselia on Routes 117, 119 and 120; Surskit on 102 and 117. Each took a slot held by a species that appears on many other tables, so nothing else lost its only home.

The Kanto handoff, and the difficulty toggle

Covered above — badges now count across regions, and Hard Mode moved into Options.

Tin Tower has a roof again

Upstream, Tin Tower cannot be climbed. Walking from the front door reaches 1F, 2F and eighteen tiles of 3F, then stops — Ho-Oh, the five Kimono Girls and every item above were unreachable.

The cause is structural: floors 3F through 6F each have every walkable region holding exactly one staircase, so you arrive and the only warp in your region is the one under your feet, which sends you back. Bad collision, missing stairs and wrong warp indices were each ruled out.

Since 7F, 8F and 9F are sound, 2F's up-staircase now leads to 7F. Floors 3F–6F stay unreachable exactly as they already were, so nothing is lost, and the roof, Ho-Oh, the Kimono Girls, Max Revive, Full Restore, Max Elixir and the Big Nugget are all reachable again.

Caveats

Known issues

Tin Tower 3F–6F are still dead

The staircase reroute is a stopgap, not a repair. Those four floors remain unreachable, and with them a Full Heal, two Max Potions, an Escape Rope, an Ultra Ball, a Rare Candy and a Carbos. Properly fixing it means redrawing four floors. Nothing unique is behind them — the wild tables there are Gastly and Rattata.

Ho-Oh has a second source anyway

Independent of Tin Tower, Ho-Oh and Lugia are both reachable at Navel Rock via the Mystic Ticket, so the dex was never at risk.

Ideas, not done yet

Other save-safe tweaks on the table

Same category as the coin-price change above: a numeric constant or price sitting in a script or config header, not a flag/var/species/item renumber, so any of these could be changed without touching an existing save. Listed here as options — none of these are applied.

  • The rest of the Game Corner prices — the TM room (3,500–4,500 coins) and held-item room (800–1,600 coins) in Celadon, plus the money→coin buy rate (flat 20¥/coin currently) and the 5,000-coin-per-visit purchase cap.
  • Safari Zone — the ¥500 "continue" fee, or how many Safari Balls you start with.
  • Wild encounter levels/rates on specific routes — per-slot level or rarity edits, not a table restructure.
  • Options-menu defaults — EXP RATE / CATCH RATE multiplier values, or which one is selected by default.
  • Trainer rematch levels or movesets — data-table edits per trainer, same shape as the existing HARD-rematch tables.
  • Named-gift Pokémon levels — e.g. the Eevee/Dratini gifts — as long as the species itself isn't changing.
  • Shiny odds — the base SHINY_ODDS constant, or the reroll amounts from Shiny Charm / chain fishing / DexNav chaining.
  • Charm Curator's badge thresholds — currently 4/8/12/16/24 for Catching → Exp. → Oval → Shiny → EV/IV Changer.
Where to find

Special Pokémon

Everything that isn't just wild grass: legendaries, mythicals, gifts, fossils, Game Corner prizes and the roamers. Every location here was read out of this ROM's own scripts, so it describes this build rather than the retail games.

Everything else

Ordinary wild Pokémon

All 417 species in this build have a reachable path, checked automatically on every build, so nothing in the dex is a dead end. Three things worth knowing:

  • Starters are catchable. Each region's first route carries all three of its own starters at roughly 10% each, so the two you turned down are still yours. Filter by Starter above.
  • Trade evolutions work offline — use a Linking Cord instead of a second console.
  • There are no version exclusives to chase. There is no paired version, so everything lives somewhere in these three regions.
